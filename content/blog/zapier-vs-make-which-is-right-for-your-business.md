## The most common automation question we get

Almost every time we start working with a new client, the same question comes up: "Should we use Zapier or Make?"

Both are excellent tools. Both can connect hundreds of apps and automate complex workflows. But they have different strengths, and choosing the wrong one can mean months of frustration and unnecessary cost.

Here is our honest take.

## What Zapier is good at

Zapier is the most beginner-friendly automation tool available. If you have never built an automation before, Zapier is the easiest place to start.

Its interface is clean and simple. You choose a trigger (something that starts the automation), choose an action (something that happens as a result), and connect them. For many straightforward automations, this is all you need.

**Zapier works best when:**

- You need to connect two or three apps with a simple trigger and action
- You want something built quickly without much technical knowledge
- You are using popular tools like Gmail, Slack, HubSpot, or Salesforce
- Your automations do not change much once they are set up

**The downside:** Zapier gets expensive quickly. Once you move beyond simple automations, the pricing can become difficult to justify for a small business. Complex logic, multiple branches, and high task volumes can push costs up significantly.

## What Make is good at

Make (formerly Integromat) is more powerful than Zapier, but it has a steeper learning curve. It uses a visual canvas interface where you can build complex workflows with multiple branches, conditional logic, and data transformations.

If Zapier is a straight line from A to B, Make is a map.

**Make works best when:**

- Your automations have complex logic with multiple conditions and branches
- You need to manipulate data as it flows between tools
- You are running high volumes of tasks and cost is a concern
- You want more control over exactly what happens at each step

**The downside:** Make takes longer to learn. For someone with no technical background, the interface can be overwhelming at first. You also need to be more careful about error handling.

## Our honest recommendation

For most small businesses starting out with automation, we recommend Zapier for simple, single-purpose workflows and Make for anything more complex.

In practice, many of our clients end up using both. Zapier handles the quick wins. Make handles the multi-step processes where data transformation and complex logic are needed.

The best tool is the one that gets your automation built and running reliably. If you are spending more time building the automation than it would save you to do the task manually, something has gone wrong.

## When neither tool is the answer

Sometimes the right answer is neither Zapier nor Make. For businesses with very specific requirements, custom-built integrations using APIs can be more reliable, more flexible, and cheaper to run at scale.

We assess this on every project. If we think a custom solution serves you better, we will say so.
